Datum Weight and Balance

Load the airplane — passengers, baggage, and fuel — and watch the weight and center of gravity stay inside the envelope. Configure the tanks, seat rows, and baggage bays to match your aircraft.

⚠ Verify before you fly

The preloaded profiles use typical / sample figures for each model. Empty weight, arms, limits, and the envelope vary by individual airplane and change with equipment and repairs. Always verify every figure against your specific aircraft's POH/AFM and its own weight-and-balance paperwork before any real flight. Every number in this app is editable for exactly that reason. This tool is for planning and learning — it does not replace the POH.

Your aircraft's actual figures User-entered

Public registries list the owner, make, and model — they do not publish empty weight or empty CG, and Datum never fills those in from a lookup. Enter them here from your aircraft's own POH/AFM weighing record. Saved records stay on this device only, and entering the same tail number again loads them automatically.

CG envelope corners (advanced — edit the approved envelope)

Each corner is a CG (in) and weight (lb) pair, ordered around the envelope shape from the POH chart. Add or remove corners to match your aircraft's chart.

2 · Fuel tanks

Set each tank's capacity and arm, then slide to how much is actually on board. Weight uses the lb/gal figure above (avgas ≈ 6.0, Jet A ≈ 6.7).

3 · Seats & passengers

Each row has its own arm and seat count — add rows or seats to match the cabin. Enter each person's actual weight (clothing included); tap a preset or type a weight. Empty seats count as zero.

4 · Baggage & cargo

Yes — baggage matters, twice over: it adds weight, and aft bays sit far behind the CG, so a heavy bag can push you out the back of the envelope even under gross. Each bay has its own arm and placard limit.

5 · Visual loading Drag & drop

The fast way to load on the ramp: drag a person or a bag from the tray onto a seat or baggage bay. Drag a seated person or a loaded bag off the aircraft (or back to the tray) to remove it, or drag it to another seat or bay to move it. Works with mouse and touch.

People drop at 180 lb and each bag adds 25 lb. For exact weights use the number fields in sections 3 and 4 — the diagram and the numbers stay in sync both ways.

6 · Trip legs Multi-leg

Plan the trip leg by leg. Each leg burns fuel, so the next leg takes off at the previous leg's landing weight — weight and CG are rechecked at every stop. One leg works exactly as the old single trip burn did. Add a leg for each stop; use gallons directly, or flight time × burn rate. Departure and destination codes are checked against real airport data as you type — ICAO (KTEB) and FAA/IATA (TEB) codes both work. With both airports valid, each leg also gets a still-air burn estimate from its distance and the profile's typical cruise speed and fuel flow (section 1) — set a leg's burn mode to “Estimate from distance” to use it, or keep entering burn manually. Every estimated figure is labeled as an estimate. The estimates are always checked against the fuel on board, whatever burn mode a leg is in: if a leg's estimated burn is more than the fuel remaining at the start of that leg — or the trip's estimates add up to more than the tanks hold — Datum warns you in the status box, on the leg, and on the load sheet.

Route map

Enter valid airport codes for a leg — TEB or KTEB both work — and the route appears here. Legs with an unknown code at either end are left off the map until fixed.

Airport names and positions come from a bundled copy of the public-domain OurAirports dataset, so checking works with no signal; when you're online, codes are also checked against the NWS Aviation Weather Center. Map: Esri, HERE, Garmin, OpenStreetMap contributors, and the GIS user community — offline, the same route is drawn as a schematic instead.
